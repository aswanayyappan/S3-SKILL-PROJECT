1. The End-to-End System Pipeline

Follow the journey of electrical energy and telemetry from sunlight photons falling on the solar panel to edge AI reasoning in the browser.

01
HARDWARE GENERATION 6V Monocrystalline PV Panel

Photovoltaic Harvesting: Sun → 6.0V DC Electricity

Sunlight carries photons that strike the silicon crystals of the 6V / 5W–10W monocrystalline solar panel, knocking electrons free (the Photovoltaic Effect). This creates a direct electrical potential difference of ~6.0V DC. Why 6V? Because a lithium-ion battery requires 4.20V to reach full charge; a 6V solar panel provides the necessary +1.8V headroom above the battery voltage so the charging controller can operate efficiently even during partly cloudy conditions.

02
CHARGE REGULATION TP4056 Module + 3.7V 18650 Li-ion Cell

Safe Chemical Energy Storage: CC/CV Charging & DW01A Protection

The raw 6V from the solar panel flows into the TP4056 charging IC. The TP4056 executes a precision two-stage Constant Current / Constant Voltage (CC/CV) charging profile, charging the 3.7V 18650 Li-ion cell at up to 1.0A until it safely terminates at 4.20V ±1%. The onboard DW01A protection IC and dual MOSFET switch protect against over-discharge (<2.5V cut-off), short circuits, and reverse polarity, ensuring the lithium battery never suffers thermal runaway.

03
DC-DC CONVERSION MT3608 Step-Up Boost Converter

Power Rail Boosting: 3.7V Battery → Stable 5.0V DC Output

As the 18650 battery discharges during use, its terminal voltage gradually drops from 4.2V down to 3.2V. Because our DC loads require a steady 5V DC supply, the MT3608 boost converter uses a 1.2MHz high-frequency switching inductor to continuously step up the varying battery voltage to a rock-solid, regulated 5.0V DC power rail.

04
ELECTRICAL LOADS DC LED Fixture & DC Motor

Dual Appliance Actuation: Lighting & Dynamic Inductive Motive Power

The 5.0V boost output powers two real hardware loads: (1) DC LED Fixture: a constant resistive lighting load drawing 0.393A (~1.82W); and (2) 5V DC Motor: a dynamic inductive load drawing 0.18A–0.45A during normal running, but pulling an initial 1.85A startup inrush surge when energized, and exceeding 2.5A during a locked-rotor stall fault.

05
ELECTRICAL ISOLATION RULE Independent External Supply for ESP32

Power Isolation: Clean Logic Rail vs Noisy Solar Load Rail

Strict Architecture Principle: The solar battery and 5V booster rail STRICTLY power the LED and Motor loads. The ESP32 microcontroller is powered via an independent external 5V supply. Why? Electric motors produce massive electromagnetic inductive kickback (back-EMF) and instantaneous voltage sags during startup. If the ESP32 shared the motor's power rail, these voltage drops would brown out the microcontroller, causing continuous Wi-Fi disconnects and system resets. Separating the rails guarantees 100% telemetry uptime.

06
SENSING & CALIBRATION ACS712 (Hall) + 5:1 Dividers + ESP32 ADC1

Precision Sensing & Zero-Bias Clamping Filter

Two ACS712-05B Hall-effect sensors measure current in series (LED: GPIO 34, Motor: GPIO 35), and two 5:1 precision resistor dividers measure voltage in parallel (LED: GPIO 32, Motor: GPIO 33). The ESP32's 12-bit ADC converts these to digital values (0–4095). Zero-Bias Rectification: Breadboard noise causes idle pins to float at ~1.18V and ACS712 sensors to register ~0.20A quiescent drift when loads are completely off. Our firmware and backend apply a digital deadband filter: if voltage is ≤ 2.0V or current is ≤ 0.28A/0.22A, readings are mathematically clamped to exact 0.00V, 0.00A, and 0.00W.

07
CLOUD TELEMETRY & CALCULUS Firebase RTDB + Node.js Engine

Cloud Ingestion, Trapezoidal Energy Integration & Electricity Billing

The ESP32 streams JSON payloads to Firebase Realtime Database (RTDB) via 2.4GHz Wi-Fi every 1–2 seconds. The Node.js cloud server reads this stream, computes instantaneous power using P = V × I, calculates cumulative energy using Trapezoidal Numerical Integration (accounting for exact millisecond intervals Δt), and multiplies total units (kWh) by the commercial tariff rate of ₹8.00 per Unit to provide real-time rupee electricity billing.

08
NEURAL AI & CONTINUOUS LEARNING PyTorch Transformer + FreeTheAI / Groq

Edge Neural Diagnostics, Continuous Self-Learning & AI Copilot Grounding

The cloud backend runs S3ElectricalTransformer, a deep PyTorch neural sequence model utilizing multi-head self-attention on sliding windows of voltage, current, power, and dI/dt. It classifies appliance states into Normal Running, Startup Inrush Surge, or Motor Stall. Every 5 minutes, an autonomous background cycle fine-tunes the PyTorch model on newly ingested operational data using an experience replay buffer (97.22% validation accuracy). When users query the AI Copilot, live telemetry is grounded into FreeTheAI (GLM-5.3) and Groq (LLaMA 3.3) LLMs via the Model Context Protocol (MCP).

2. Complete Hardware Component Catalog (All 9 Physical Devices)

Detailed breakdown of each physical component: exact model, specifications, pin connections, operating principle, and failure impacts.

PRIMARY GENERATOR

6V 5W–10W Monocrystalline PV Panel

6V DC
Pins: Red Lead (+) → TP4056 IN+ • Black Lead (-) → TP4056 IN-

Purpose: Primary clean renewable energy generator. Converts photons into direct electrical current.
Working Principle: Semiconductor p-n junction absorbs light; photons transfer energy to valence electrons, generating an electric field.
Why 6V? Provides the +1.8V headroom needed above the 4.2V lithium-ion charge cutoff threshold.
Failure Mode: In nighttime or disconnection, system seamlessly draws stored power from the 18650 battery.

BATTERY CONTROLLER

TP4056 Lithium Charge & Protection Module

1.0A CC/CV
Pins: IN+/IN- (Solar) • B+/B- (18650 Battery) • OUT+/OUT- (Booster IN)

Purpose: Safe battery charging and multi-fault protection.
Working Principle: Regulates current to 1A in Constant Current mode until battery reaches 4.2V, then switches to Constant Voltage mode with auto-cutoff.
Protection ICs: DW01A protection IC with dual 8205A MOSFETs disconnects load if voltage drops below 2.5V (over-discharge) or shorts.
Failure Mode: Disconnecting TP4056 stops battery charging and removes protection against over-voltage damage.

ENERGY STORAGE

3.7V 18650 Rechargeable Li-ion Cell

2200–2600 mAh
Pins: Positive terminal → TP4056 B+ • Negative terminal → TP4056 B-

Purpose: Electrochemical buffer storing clean solar energy for round-the-clock continuous load operation.
Working Principle: Lithium ions move between graphite anode and metal-oxide cathode during charge/discharge cycles.
Nominal Specs: 3.7V nominal, 4.2V peak charged, 3.0V discharge cutoff.
Failure Mode: Missing battery means loads only run during bright sun and shut off immediately when solar irradiance drops.

STEP-UP CONVERTER

MT3608 DC-DC Step-Up Boost Converter

3.7V → 5.0V
Pins: IN+/IN- from TP4056 OUT • OUT+/OUT- to 5V Monitored Load Rail

Purpose: Steps up fluctuating battery voltage (3.2V–4.2V) to a rock-solid, regulated 5.0V DC bus.
Working Principle: Internal power MOSFET switches at 1.2MHz, charging an inductor and discharging it into an output capacitor via a Schottky diode.
Why Needed? 5V DC Motor and LED require a steady 5V. Connecting them directly to the 3.7V battery would cause motor stalling and LED dimming as the battery discharges.
Efficiency: Up to 93% energy conversion efficiency.

EDGE MCU

ESP32 DevKit V1 (Tensilica Dual-Core)

240MHz • Wi-Fi
Pins: GPIO 32 (LED V) • GPIO 34 (LED I) • GPIO 33 (Motor V) • GPIO 35 (Motor I) • VIN/GND (Ext 5V)

Purpose: Central processing unit for analog multi-channel sampling, zero-bias deadband filtering, and 2.4GHz Wi-Fi cloud streaming.
Processor: Dual-core 32-bit Xtensa LX6 microcontroller running at 240MHz with 520KB SRAM.
Powering: Powered from an independent external 5V supply, completely isolated from load transients.
ADC Architecture: Uses ADC1 channels only, ensuring analog readings function seamlessly alongside active Wi-Fi transmission.

CURRENT SENSING

ACS712-05B Hall-Effect Current Sensor Modules (Dual)

±5A • 185mV/A
Pins: Screw terminals: In-series with (+) wire • VCC: 5V, GND: GND • OUT: GPIO 34 (LED), GPIO 35 (Motor)

Purpose: Precise, galvanically isolated current measurement for both loads.
Working Principle: Current passes through internal copper conduction path, creating a magnetic field proportional to current. An internal Hall element detects the field and outputs an analog voltage: Vout = 2.5V + (I × 0.185V/A).
Zero Current Output: 2.50V (half of VCC) represents zero amperes.
Why ACS712? Eliminates ground loops and heat loss associated with traditional shunt resistors.

VOLTAGE SENSING

5:1 Precision Resistor Divider Modules (Dual)

0–25V Input
Pins: Screw terminals: Across load (+) & (-) • S: GPIO 32 (LED), GPIO 33 (Motor) • -: GND

Purpose: Scales high DC voltages (up to 25V) down to safe levels for the 3.3V ESP32 ADC.
Resistor Network: 30 kΩ and 7.5 kΩ in series. Vout = Vin × (7.5 / 37.5) = Vin / 5.
Why Essential? Connecting a 5V–12V DC power rail directly to an ESP32 GPIO pin would immediately destroy the internal silicon gate. The divider keeps all signals strictly ≤ 3.3V.

LIGHTING APPLIANCE

5V DC LED Lighting Fixture (Load 1)

5V • 0.393A • 1.82W
Pins: Positive: Boost 5V Rail via ACS712 Module 1 • Negative: Common GND

Purpose: Primary baseline lighting appliance demonstrating steady-state energy telemetry.
Electrical Behavior: Non-inductive resistive diode load. Exhibits zero inrush transients and flat continuous power consumption (~1.82W).
Telemetry Channel: Monitored in real-time by GPIO 32 (Voltage) and GPIO 34 (Current).

DYNAMIC INDUCTIVE LOAD

5V DC Motor / Pump Appliance (Load 2)

5V • 0.18A–2.5A
Pins: Positive: Boost 5V Rail via ACS712 Module 2 • Negative: Common GND

Purpose: Dynamic motive power appliance demonstrating complex electrical transients, motor inrush surges, and stall fault detection.
Operating Phases:
• Startup Inrush Surge: Draws 1.85A peak for ~420ms when rotor accelerates from rest.
• Normal Running: Settles to 0.18A–0.45A once back-EMF is established.
• Stall / Overload Fault: If shaft is blocked, current exceeds 2.5A, triggering PyTorch anomaly alerts.

3. Software, Mathematics & Neural Network Architecture

The algorithms, mathematical equations, and PyTorch deep learning models that power the real-time electrical analytics.

MATHEMATICAL FORMULA

Strict DC Power Formula: P = V × I

Watts (W)

In Direct Current (DC) circuits, voltage and current are strictly in phase without reactive displacement (Power Factor = 1.0). True instantaneous power is calculated directly:
P_instant = V_measured × I_measured
Total monitored load power is the sum: P_total = P_led + P_motor.

CALCULUS INTEGRATION

Trapezoidal Energy Integration (kWh)

kWh Units

Power varies dynamically over time. To calculate total energy, the engine integrates power over exact millisecond timestamps using the Trapezoidal Rule:
ΔE = ((P_prev + P_curr) / 2) × (Δt_ms / 3,600,000,000) kWh
This rejects discrete step errors and accurately records brief motor inrush spikes.

FINANCIAL BILLING

Electricity Tariff: ₹8.00 / Unit

Rupees (₹)

One standard electrical “Unit” equals 1 kilowatt-hour (1 kWh = 1000 Watts used for 1 hour). The live bill is computed as:
Energy Charge (₹) = Cumulative Energy (kWh) × ₹8.00
Monthly projected bill is extrapolated from the running 60-minute consumption moving average.

SENSOR RECTIFICATION

Zero-Bias Deadband Calibration Filter

Noise Clamping

The Problem: When loads are turned off, unshielded breadboard ADC pins float at ~1.18V, and Hall sensors drift by ~0.20A. Left unchecked, this false phantom power accumulates into incorrect electricity bills.
The Solution: The engine applies deadband clamping:
if (V ≤ 2.0V) { V = 0.00; }
if (I_led ≤ 0.28A) { I_led = 0.00; P_led = 0.00; }
if (I_motor ≤ 0.22A) { I_motor = 0.00; P_motor = 0.00; }

DEEP LEARNING

PyTorch Transformer (S3ElectricalTransformer)

Multi-Head Attention

A 4-layer PyTorch sequence transformer trained on 8-step time windows of [Voltage, Current, Power, dI/dt]. Multi-head self-attention models temporal dynamics, distinguishing between safe motor startup surges (420ms transient) and dangerous locked-rotor motor stalls (>2.5A sustained).

AUTONOMOUS MLOPS

Continuous Learning & Replay Buffer Loop

Auto-Retrain 5m

New telemetry cycles are automatically appended to a sliding experience replay buffer. Every 5 minutes, an autonomous background pass fine-tunes the PyTorch neural network weights using AdamW optimizer and cross-entropy loss, maintaining 97.22% validation accuracy without human intervention.

4. The Project Dictionary (“Small Words” Explained Simply)

Every technical term, acronym, and electrical word defined in clear, accessible everyday language with analogies.

ADC (Analog-to-Digital Converter) Hardware
Simple Analogy: An interpreter that translates a continuous analog ruler measurement into a digital typed number for a computer.

The internal circuitry on the ESP32 that measures continuous physical voltage levels (0.0V to 3.3V) and converts them into 12-bit digital numbers (integers from 0 to 4095) so code can analyze them.

GPIO (General Purpose Input/Output) Hardware
Simple Analogy: The physical ports or sockets on a microchip where wires are plugged in to communicate with the outside world.

The programmable metal pins on the ESP32 (e.g. GPIO 32, 33, 34, 35) configured to read incoming analog voltages from the sensors or send signals to relays and LEDs.

Photovoltaic (PV) Effect Physics
Simple Analogy: Sunlight shining on silicon acts like pool balls hitting electrons, knocking them forward through a wire to create electricity.

The physical phenomenon where light particles (photons) strike semiconductor silicon cells, transferring energy to electrons and generating a direct current (DC) electrical voltage.

CC/CV Charging Battery
Simple Analogy: Pumping water into a balloon rapidly at first (Constant Current), then slowing down to a gentle trickle near the end so it doesn't pop (Constant Voltage).

The standard safe charging profile for lithium batteries used by the TP4056: feeds a steady 1.0A current until the cell reaches 4.2V, then caps voltage at 4.2V and lets current taper to zero.

Hall Effect Sensing
Simple Analogy: Measuring water speed in a hose by listening to how fast an external wheel spins without cutting into the hose itself.

When current flows through a conductor, it creates a magnetic field. The ACS712 senses this magnetic field perpendicular to current flow, generating a voltage proportional to amperes without direct electrical contact.

DC-DC Step-Up (Boost) Converter Power
Simple Analogy: A bicycle pump that takes low-pressure air strokes and compresses them into high-pressure tire inflation.

An electronic circuit that takes a lower input DC voltage (3.2V–4.2V from the battery) and boosts it up to a higher, stable output voltage (5.0V DC) using high-frequency switching and an inductor.

Deadband / Clamping Filter Software
Simple Analogy: Noise cancellation in headphones: ignoring faint background room rustle so you only hear true voice conversations.

A software algorithm that forces any sensor measurement below a predefined threshold (e.g. ≤ 2.0V or ≤ 0.22A) to exact zero, eliminating phantom sensor drift when appliances are switched off.

Inrush Current (Startup Surge) Electrical
Simple Analogy: Pushing a heavy stalled car: you need a massive initial shove to get it rolling, after which it rolls easily with gentle effort.

The brief, normal spike in electrical current (e.g. 1.85A for 420ms on our DC motor) when an electric motor first starts from a complete standstill before the rotor generates opposing back-EMF.

Motor Stall (Locked Rotor) Fault
Simple Analogy: Stepping on the gas pedal while holding the handbrake: the engine strains dangerously and overheats because the wheels cannot turn.

A severe fault where the motor shaft is physically jammed. Because no back-EMF is generated, current rises uncontrollably (>2.5A), creating fire and coil burn risks if not detected by AI.

Kilowatt-Hour (kWh) & Unit Measurement
Simple Analogy: Like measuring total liters of water pumped out of a tank over an entire month.

The standard commercial unit of electrical energy. 1 kWh (or 1 Unit) equals 1,000 Watts of electrical power consumed continuously for 1 hour: 1 kWh = 3,600,000 Joules.

Electricity Tariff Rate (₹8.00 / Unit) Billing
Simple Analogy: The price per liter of fuel at a gas station: multiply liters used by price per liter to get total bill.

The pricing schedule set by electrical utility providers. In our system, each 1.0 kWh of energy consumed costs exactly ₹8.00.

Back-EMF (Counter-Electromotive Force) Physics
Simple Analogy: Running into a headwind: the faster you run, the more air pushes back against you, naturally capping your top speed.

The opposing voltage naturally generated by a spinning electric motor armature that opposes the incoming supply voltage, naturally limiting steady-state running current to ~0.2A.

Brownout & Voltage Sag Power Quality
Simple Analogy: When someone flushes a toilet in the house and the shower pressure suddenly drops to a trickle.

A sudden temporary dip in voltage caused by high startup load current. If microcontrollers experience a brownout below ~2.7V, their internal logic crashes and restarts.

Model Context Protocol (MCP) AI / Protocols
Simple Analogy: A standardized universal plug that lets AI bots like ChatGPT reach into local devices and read dials or push buttons safely.

An open standard protocol created by Anthropic allowing AI Large Language Models to call structured software tools (like get_live_telemetry) and access real-time hardware state without hallucination.

Experience Replay Buffer Machine Learning
Simple Analogy: A student's flashcard review pile: reviewing both new test questions and previous flashcards so they never forget past knowledge.

A circular memory buffer that stores historical telemetry transitions. During continuous learning passes, the neural network samples from both old and new data to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

Common Star Ground (GND) Circuit Design
Simple Analogy: Sea level: the universal zero-height baseline from which all mountain heights and ocean depths are measured.

Tying all ground wires from the solar charger, battery booster, sensors, and ESP32 to a single central reference point, ensuring 0.0V is identical across all components to eliminate ground loops.

5. Viva, Defense & Evaluator Technical Q&A

The top engineering questions asked by project reviewers, university professors, and judges — answered with rock-solid technical justifications.

Q1 Why is the ESP32 powered from an independent external supply instead of the solar booster rail?
Answer: Electric motors are highly inductive loads that generate severe startup inrush surges (1.85A) and back-EMF noise. If the ESP32 shared the 5V booster rail with the motor, these transient load surges would cause sudden voltage sags (brownouts) dropping below 2.7V, which crashes the ESP32 and resets Wi-Fi. Powering the ESP32 from an independent external 5V rail isolates sensitive logic from motor noise and guarantees uninterrupted monitoring.
Q2 Why use a 6V solar panel to charge a 3.7V battery? Why not a 3.7V or 5V panel?
Answer: A single lithium-ion 18650 cell reaches full charge at 4.20V. Linear charge management ICs like the TP4056 require an input voltage at least 0.3V–0.5V higher than the target battery voltage (dropout voltage). A 6V panel provides ~6.0V under load, ensuring continuous charging even during morning, evening, or cloudy hours when solar irradiance drops.
Q3 Why is the MT3608 boost converter needed between the battery and the loads?
Answer: A lithium battery's output voltage is not constant; it drops from 4.2V when fully charged down to 3.0V as it discharges. If connected directly, the LED would dim and the DC motor would lose torque and stall as the battery drains. The MT3608 boost converter steps up this changing 3.0V–4.2V input to a rock-solid, regulated 5.0V output, ensuring loads always operate at full rated power.
Q4 How does the PyTorch neural network distinguish a safe startup inrush surge from a dangerous motor stall?
Answer: By analyzing temporal sequences rather than single scalar values. A normal startup surge is transient: current peaks at 1.85A but rapidly decays within 420 milliseconds as rotor back-EMF develops. A locked-rotor motor stall produces a sustained high current exceeding 2.5A with zero back-EMF decay. The S3ElectricalTransformer multi-head attention mechanism models both current magnitude and rate of change (dI/dt) over time, classifying inrush as benign and stall as critical.
Q5 What causes the 1.18V and 0.2A sensor readings when loads are off, and how does zero-bias rectification fix it?
Answer: High-impedance breadboard traces act as antennas picking up stray electromagnetic noise, causing floating ADC pins to read ~1.18V. Additionally, the ACS712 Hall-effect sensor outputs 2.50V at 0A, and minor thermal drift or VCC variance causes a ~0.20A offset. Our zero-bias deadband filter checks: if measured voltage ≤ 2.0V or current ≤ 0.28A, the software clamps both to exact 0.00V, 0.00A, and 0.00W, preventing false billing accumulation.
Q6 Why use an ACS712 Hall-effect sensor instead of a low-cost shunt resistor?
Answer: An ACS712 sensor provides complete galvanic isolation up to 2.1 kV between the high-current power path and the low-voltage ESP32 sensing pins. Shunt resistors require direct conductive insertion into the circuit, generate resistive I²R heat losses, and cause ground loop voltage offsets that distort sensitive ADC readings.
